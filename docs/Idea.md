## Details

- **[a]** In the past practice of learnware research, the specification of a model (or a task) is generated by the parameter shift of an auxilary model(here we call it judge model) to represent the ability of the model. It is just like, there's the very judge who judges everything so that all the models are assessed solely by the very one person, and thus the perception(parameter shift) it has is just to all the models. But this approach requires on-the-spot training and only dicusses the selection across models. If we make things different, that it's not A thinks B is ..., thinks C is ..., thinks D is ..., and we rate by A's score ,anymore but that B thinks in this situation, it can do..., C thinks it can do..., D thinks it can do,... and they cooperate to seek out the best arragement of who's acting and who's not, who should act first, and then who's next. This might be solved by multi-agent reinforcement learning, which will optimize actions in a system of agents, but the details is tricky and we've not decided yet...
